# ./
At the most basic level, meteorites can be divided into stony, irons and stony-irons. Stony meteorites (by far the largest group) can be divided into chondrites (very small asteroids) and achondrited (planets and large asteroids).

# ./Achondrites
Achondrites are meteorites that lack chondrules, meaning they formed from melted or partially melted material on differentiated parent bodies rather than from unaltered solar nebula dust. They represent igneous rocks from asteroids, Mars, and the Moon, and are far rarer than chondrites.
It is thought that on early asteroids larger that around 100km, there was enough heat being generated mostly from 26Al to partly or completely melt them.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achondrite

# ./Achondrites/HED
The Howardite-Eucrite-Diogenite clan is the largest achondrite group and is widely believed to originate from the asteroid 4 Vesta. Together its members sample Vesta's crust (eucrites), mantle (diogenites), and mixed surface regolith (howardites).

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HED_meteorite

# ./Achondrites/HED/Diogenite
Diogenites are coarse-grained orthopyroxenite cumulates thought to represent deep crustal or upper mantle rock from Vesta, formed by the slow cooling and crystal settling of magma.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogenite

# ./Achondrites/HED/Eucrite
Eucrites are basaltic achondrites resembling terrestrial basalt, composed mainly of calcium-rich pyroxene and plagioclase feldspar. They represent surface lava flows and shallow intrusions on Vesta's crust.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucrite

# ./Achondrites/HED/Eucrite/Dhofar 007 (Eucrite-Cumulate) - 1.09g
This Omani find is a cumulate eucrite, a slowly cooled, coarser-grained variety formed deep within a magma chamber rather than in a fast-cooling surface flow, giving it larger, better-formed crystals than typical basaltic eucrites.

# ./Achondrites/HED/Howardite
Howardites are polymict breccias made of mixed eucrite and diogenite fragments, representing regolith gardened by impacts on Vesta's surface. Their brecciated texture distinguishes them from the more uniform eucrites and diogenites.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howardite

# ./Achondrites/Ungrouped
These achondrites have distinctive mineralogical or isotopic signatures that don't match any established clan, each likely representing a unique, otherwise unsampled parent body.

# ./Achondrites/Martian
Martian meteorites are igneous rocks blasted off the surface of Mars by asteroid impacts. They are identified by trapped gases matching the Martian atmosphere as measured by spacecraft.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martian_meteorite

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Shergottite
Shergottites are the most common Martian meteorite type, ranging from basaltic to lherzolitic in composition, and typically have surprisingly young crystallization ages relative to most of Mars' geologic history.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shergottites

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Shergottite/augite basalt
This subtype of basaltic shergottite is dominated by the pyroxene augite, representing rapidly cooled lava that reached the Martian surface.

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Shergottite/basaltic
Basaltic shergottites are fine-grained igneous rocks closely resembling terrestrial basalt, formed from lava flows erupted onto the Martian surface.

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Shergottite/gabbroic
Gabbroic shergottites are coarser-grained than their basaltic counterparts, having cooled more slowly, and are thought to represent shallow intrusive bodies rather than surface flows.

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Shergottite/unclassified
These shergottites have not yet been assigned to a specific textural or mineralogical subtype, often due to limited sample size or incomplete study.

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Shergottite/olivine phyric
Olivine-phyric shergottites contain large, early-formed olivine crystals suspended in a finer groundmass, recording a magma that carried older crystals up from depth before erupting.

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Shergottite/poikilitic
Poikilitic shergottites have a distinctive cumulate texture in which large pyroxene crystals enclose smaller olivine grains, indicating slow cooling at depth within a magma body.

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Chassignite
Chassignites are extremely rare dunites, composed almost entirely of olivine, thought to represent cumulate rock from the Martian mantle or lower crust. Only a handful are known.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chassignites

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Nakhlite
Nakhlites are clinopyroxene-rich igneous rocks from Martian lava flows that show clear evidence of alteration by liquid water, making them important for studying Mars' aqueous history.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhlite

# ./Achondrites/Martian/Polymict breccia
These Martian breccias combine fragments of multiple rock types cemented together, representing mixed and impact-processed material from the Martian regolith.

# ./Achondrites/Lunar
Lunar meteorites are rocks ejected from the Moon by impacts, identified by matching their mineralogy and isotopes to samples returned by the Apollo and Luna missions.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_meteorite

# ./Achondrites/Lunar/gabbro
Lunar gabbros are coarse-grained intrusive rocks composed of plagioclase and pyroxene, representing slowly cooled magma beneath the lunar surface.

# ./Achondrites/Lunar/troctolitic ...
Troctolitic rocks are composed chiefly of olivine and plagioclase, sourced from deeper portions of the lunar crust and offering insight into the Moon's early differentiation.

# ./Achondrites/Lunar/ferroan anorthosite
Ferroan anorthosites are plagioclase-rich rocks that crystallized from the Moon's primordial magma ocean, making them among the oldest known lunar crustal materials.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferroan_anorthosite

# ./Achondrites/Lunar/melt breccia
Lunar melt breccias formed when the heat of large impacts partially melted surrounding rock, welding fragments together in a once-molten matrix.

# ./Achondrites/Lunar/basaltic breccia
These breccias consist of fragmented mare basalt pieces fused together by impact processes on the lunar surface.

# ./Achondrites/Lunar/felspathic beccia
Feldspathic breccias are dominated by plagioclase feldspar and derive from the ancient, aluminum-rich lunar highlands crust.

# ./Achondrites/Lunar/mare basalt
Mare basalts are iron- and titanium-rich lavas that filled the Moon's dark basaltic plains, distinct in composition from the brighter highlands material.

# ./Achondrites/Lunar/fragmental breccia
Fragmental breccias consist of angular, largely unmelted rock and mineral fragments from the lunar regolith, cemented together without significant impact melt.

# ./Achondrites/Evolved
Evolved achondrites come from parent asteroids that experienced enough melting to produce distinct igneous minerals, but retain chemical signatures linking them to a chondritic starting composition.

# ./Achondrites/Evolved/Angrite
Angrites are among the oldest known igneous rocks in the solar system, with unusual calcium-aluminum-titanium-rich mineralogy unlike any other achondrite group.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angrite

# ./Achondrites/Evolved/Ureilite
Ureilites are carbon-rich, olivine-pyroxene achondrites containing graphite and sometimes diamond, thought to be mantle residues left after partial melting of a carbon-bearing parent body.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ureilite

# ./Achondrites/Evolved/Aubrites
Aubrites are highly reduced, enstatite-rich achondrites that closely resemble terrestrial enstatite rocks, likely related to the same reduced nebular material that formed enstatite chondrites.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aubrite

# ./Achondrites/Primitive
Primitive achondrites experienced only partial melting, leaving them chemically close to chondrites while showing igneous textures, making them a bridge between the two major meteorite classes.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_achondrite

# ./Achondrites/Primitive/Acapulcoite
Acapulcoites are fine-grained primitive achondrites that underwent only minor melting, preserving a composition close to that of ordinary chondrites.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acapulcoite

# ./Achondrites/Primitive/Brachinite
Brachinites are olivine-dominated primitive achondrites interpreted as residues left behind after a parent body's basaltic component was partially melted and removed.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachinite

# ./Achondrites/Primitive/Winonaite
Winonaites are the silicate counterpart to IAB iron meteorites, representing the stony portion of a metal-silicate parent body that never fully differentiated.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winonaite

# ./Achondrites/Primitive/Lodranite
Lodranites are coarser-grained and more heavily melted than acapulcoites, representing a later stage of partial melting on the same or a related parent body.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodranite

# ./Chondrites
Chondrites are the most common meteorites and among the most primitive materials in the solar system, preserving chondrules and matrix largely unaltered since their formation 4.56 billion years ago.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chondrite

# ./Chondrites/R
Rumuruti chondrites are a rare, highly oxidized group distinct from ordinary chondrites, with unusually iron-rich silicates and a strongly brecciated texture.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chondrite#Rumuruti_chondrites

# ./Chondrites/E
Enstatite chondrites formed under highly reducing conditions in the early solar system and are dominated by the mineral enstatite, with isotopic compositions remarkably similar to Earth's.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enstatite_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/E/EL
EL chondrites are the lower-iron subgroup of enstatite chondrites, containing less free metal than their EH counterparts.

# ./Chondrites/E/EH
EH chondrites are the higher-iron, higher-metal subgroup of enstatite chondrites, formed under even more reducing conditions.

# ./Chondrites/OC
Ordinary chondrites are the most abundant class of meteorites recovered on Earth, subdivided by bulk iron content into the H, L, and LL groups.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/OC/L
L chondrites have a "low" total iron content compared to H chondrites, with iron split between metal and silicate minerals.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/OC/L/L5
Petrologic type 5 indicates L chondrites that experienced significant thermal metamorphism on their parent body, largely erasing original chondrule boundaries.

# ./Chondrites/OC/L/L6
Petrologic type 6 marks the most thermally metamorphosed L chondrites, with recrystallized textures and chondrules that are difficult to distinguish.

# ./Chondrites/OC/L/L3
Petrologic type 3 L chondrites are the least metamorphosed, retaining sharp, well-defined chondrules and primitive matrix material.

# ./Chondrites/OC/L/L4
Petrologic type 4 L chondrites show moderate thermal metamorphism, with chondrules still visible but matrix beginning to recrystallize.

# ./Chondrites/OC/ungrouped
These ordinary chondrites have compositions that don't cleanly fit the standard H, L, or LL classification.

# ./Chondrites/OC/H
H chondrites have "high" total iron content and are the most abundant meteorite group recovered on Earth, with abundant metallic iron-nickel grains.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/OC/H/H4
Petrologic type 4 H chondrites show moderate thermal metamorphism with chondrules still clearly visible.

# ./Chondrites/OC/H/H3
Petrologic type 3 H chondrites are minimally metamorphosed, preserving pristine, sharply defined chondrules.

# ./Chondrites/OC/H/H5
Petrologic type 5 H chondrites are more heavily recrystallized, with chondrule boundaries becoming indistinct.

# ./Chondrites/OC/unclassified
These ordinary chondrites have not yet been assigned to a specific group or petrologic type.

# ./Chondrites/OC/LL
LL chondrites have the lowest total iron and metal content of the ordinary chondrite groups, giving them a lighter overall appearance.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LL_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/OC/LL/LL5
Petrologic type 5 LL chondrites show substantial thermal metamorphism while retaining some recognizable chondrule structure.

# ./Chondrites/OC/LL/LL4
Petrologic type 4 LL chondrites show moderate metamorphism, an intermediate stage between fresh and heavily recrystallized material.

# ./Chondrites/OC/LL/LL3
Petrologic type 3 LL chondrites are the least altered, with well-preserved chondrules and primitive matrix.

# ./Chondrites/OC/LL/LL3/NWA 11234 (LL3 S2 W1) - 1.2g
This Northwest Africa find is a primitive, low-shock (S2), lightly weathered (W1) LL3 chondrite, prized for preserving fine, unaltered chondrule textures from the early solar system.

# ./Chondrites/OC/LL/LL6
Petrologic type 6 LL chondrites are the most thermally metamorphosed, with heavily recrystallized textures and faint chondrule outlines.

# ./Chondrites/CC
Carbonaceous chondrites are volatile- and carbon-rich meteorites thought to preserve some of the most pristine material from the solar nebula, including presolar grains and organic compounds.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonaceous_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/CC/CH
CH chondrites are metal-rich carbonaceous chondrites with unusually small chondrules and a high proportion of metallic iron-nickel.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CH_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/CC/CH/ISHEYEVO (CH-CBb) - 0.5g
Isheyevo is a unique breccia combining CH and CBb (Bencubbin-like) lithologies in a single stone, making it a rare window into the mixing of distinct metal-rich carbonaceous chondrite populations.

# ./Chondrites/CC/ungrouped
These carbonaceous chondrites have chemical or isotopic signatures that don't match any established CC group, each representing a distinct, otherwise unsampled parent body.

# ./Chondrites/CC/CV
CV (Vigarano-type) chondrites are known for their large chondrules and abundant calcium-aluminum-rich inclusions (CAIs), among the oldest solids formed in the solar system.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CV_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/CC/CR
CR (Renazzo-type) chondrites feature large, metal-rimmed chondrules and a hydrated matrix, indicating a parent body that experienced significant aqueous alteration.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/CC/CO
CO (Ornans-type) chondrites are characterized by small chondrules and abundant refractory inclusions, with relatively minor aqueous alteration compared to other CC groups.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CO_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/CC/CO/NWA 11536 (CO3) - 1.2g
This Northwest Africa specimen is a petrologic type 3 CO chondrite, meaning it retains its small, sharply defined chondrules largely unaltered by thermal metamorphism.

# ./Chondrites/CC/CB
CB (Bencubbin-type) chondrites are exceptionally metal-rich and are thought to have formed from an impact-melt event rather than typical nebular condensation, giving them a very unusual origin among chondrites.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CB_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/CC/CL
CL chondrites are a small, relatively recently defined carbonaceous chondrite group with chondrule sizes and oxygen isotopes distinct from other CC groups.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CL_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/CC/CM
CM (Mighei-type) chondrites are heavily aqueously altered and rich in organic compounds and hydrated minerals, making them key targets for studying the origins of prebiotic chemistry.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CM_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/CC/unclassified
These carbonaceous chondrites have not yet been assigned to a specific subgroup.

# ./Chondrites/CC/CK
CK (Karoonda-type) chondrites are notably oxidized, with olivine-rich chondrules and a coarse-grained, recrystallized matrix.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CK_chondrite

# ./Chondrites/CC/CI
CI (Ivuna-type) chondrites are the most chemically primitive meteorites known, lacking chondrules entirely and closely matching the Sun's composition, though heavily altered by water.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CI_chondrite

# ./Stony Irons
Stony-iron meteorites contain roughly equal parts metallic iron-nickel and silicate minerals, formed at the boundary between a differentiated asteroid's metallic core and rocky mantle.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony-iron_meteorite

# ./Stony Irons/Mesosiderites
Mesosiderites are breccias mixing metal and silicate fragments in roughly equal measure, likely formed by a violent collision that mingled a metallic core with crustal or mantle material.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesosiderite

# ./Stony Irons/Palasites
Pallasites are famous for their striking texture of olivine crystals embedded in a nickel-iron matrix, previously thought to have form at the core-mantle boundary of a differentiated asteroid but more likely are a result of a collision between an iron core and slilicate rich asteroid.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallasite

# ./Stony Irons/Palasites/ES
Eagle Station pallasites are a rare, chemically and isotopically distinct pallasite subgroup, separate from the more common main-group pallasites.

# ./Stony Irons/Palasites/PMG
Main-group pallasites make up the majority of known pallasites, sharing a common metal chemistry consistent with a single parent body.

# ./Irons
Iron meteorites are composed almost entirely of nickel-iron metal and represent fragments of the cores of differentiated asteroids, shattered by ancient collisions.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_meteorite

# ./Irons/IC
Group IC irons are a small, chemically coherent group of iron meteorites with distinctive nickel and trace element patterns.

# ./Irons/IAB
IAB is the largest and most chemically diverse iron meteorite group, often containing silicate inclusions, suggesting a parent body that never fully separated its metal and rock.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAB_meteorites

# ./Irons/ungrouped
These iron meteorites have chemical compositions that don't match any established group, each likely sampling a unique parent body's core.

# ./Irons/IVA
Group IVA irons are known for their fine octahedrite structure and a wide range of nickel contents, indicating slow cooling within a large metallic core.

# ./Irons/IIE
Group IIE irons often contain silicate inclusions and show evidence of complex impact and reheating history, distinguishing them from purely core-derived groups.

# ./Irons/IIIE
Group IIIE is a small, chemically distinct iron meteorite group with coarse octahedrite structure.

# ./Irons/IIIAB
IIIAB is one of the largest iron meteorite groups, with a coarse to medium octahedrite structure reflecting slow cooling in a substantial metallic core.

# ./Irons/IIAB
Group IIAB irons are characterized by a hexahedrite to coarse octahedrite structure and relatively low, tightly clustered nickel content.

Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIAB_meteorites
